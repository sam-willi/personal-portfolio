What is it?

A several-week LEGO SPIKE Prime camp for grades 3–8, written as a business plan and pitched to a sports camp that had never taught STEM.

01Context

$55,473+ profit across the first two summers, all of it funding Team 971.

Spartans Sports Camp is a 501(c)(3) affiliated with Mountain View High School athletics. It was trusted and well run, but had no STEM offering, and Bay Area robotics camps were charging $525–599 a week. Rohan Gadde and I wrote a business plan for a student-led robotics program under their brand and pitched it to the director.

02What I designed

  1. 01CurriculumThree days of building and programming challenges, then two days of competition we called Robo-Olympics.
  2. 02Kits + staffTwenty LEGO SPIKE Prime kits, one per two campers. Ten FRC Team 971 counselors a week plus two paid adult supervisors.
  3. 03MarketingI made the flyers, figured out where parents would actually run into them, and hauled our 125-lb robot to community events to pitch it in person.

03Result

Sold out all three summers, every session.

Year one: 45 campers and $19,383 profit on a volunteer staff. Year two split into beginner and advanced sessions and cleared $36,090 with paid staff, the second-highest revenue of the camp’s 13 programs. A third summer ran in 2026 after I graduated. I ran interviews, payroll and operations.

Learning by building things.
