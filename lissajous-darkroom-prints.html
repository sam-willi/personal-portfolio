What is it?

Two perpendicular waves draw a curve that disappears as soon as the sound stops. I exposed six songs onto photo paper with a laser so the shapes stayed.

01Observation

Sound moves a mirror, and the mirror steers the laser.

I was experimenting with sound visualization using a balloon, a speaker and a laser (you should try it) and got caught by Lissajous curves, the pattern two perpendicular waves draw when they interact. They were genuinely one of the most mesmerizing things I had ever seen, so I set out on a quest to capture them.

02Method

  1. 01Six songsI chose six songs with different harmonic structures, so each one draws a different pattern.
  2. 02ExposeIn the darkroom I exposed photo paper to a laser driven by a different portion of each song.
  3. 03PrintThe sound became six physical prints, one per song.

03Reflection

Exposure times took a lot of trial and error.

The physics is easy enough to look up. Getting a print that looks like what you see on the wall was the hard part. Most of this project was sitting on the darkroom floor, waiting for an exposure to finish.
